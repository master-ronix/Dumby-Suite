# ADR-015: The Developer Identity Model — Four Identifiers, Four Surfaces

**Status:** Accepted — 08/10/2026
**Deciders:** Dumby Core Engineering — Quixotic Mnemonics
**Supersedes:** ADR-014's attribution clause (which placed the @handle in the attribution line).
**Governing sections:** the title & attribution grammar (§1.5) · the manifest identity fields (§11.2) · the secured upload pipeline (§15.8) · the credentials service (§22.8) · the devId sensitivity clause (§32.12)

## Context

The renderer surfaces identity on two lines — the attribution under the title and the footer — and four identifiers were being conflated. `devId` and `buildId` are issued at different times by different services; `devName` and `devUsername` are two facets of one registered account. ADR-014 placed the @handle in the attribution line, which §1.5 reserves for the developer's name, and the loader fell back to printing the raw `devId` — which §32.12 treats as being as sensitive as a bot token.

## Decision

Four identifiers, four surfaces:

- **`devId`** — the registered developer identifier, issued by the Credentials Service at developer registration (§15.6). It is **never rendered** on a user-facing surface; an unregistered `devId` resolves to the framework developer, never to the identifier.
- **`buildId`** — the identifier of one uploaded version, assigned by Swastik per accepted version (§15.8). It appears in the footer.
- **`devName`** — the developer's display name. It appears in the attribution line: `Developed by <devName>` (§1.5).
- **`devUsername`** — the developer's registered @handle. It appears in the footer: `<buildId> · @<devUsername>`.

Both `devName` and `devUsername` are resolved from the account registry by the manifest's `devId` (the registry seam is `developer-registry.ts`), so neither is a static string. The version is not shown on any surface (ADR-013).
