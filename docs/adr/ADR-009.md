# ADR-009: Title & Attribution Grammar — A4 Adopted as the Single Grammar

**Status:** Accepted — 18/09/2026
**Deciders:** Dumby Core Engineering — Quixotic Mnemonics
**Supersedes:** The A1 title/attribution guidance, superseded by this adoption.
**Governing sections:** blueprint.md 5.0.0 §16.5, §20 · interface.md 5.0.0 (title & attribution) · master-prompt.md 5.0.0

## Context

The two source references disagree on how dashboard and surface titles carry identity and
attribution. The raw requirements (A1) imply one treatment; the interface reference (A4,
the 24 July 2026 window) specifies another. The program's standing constraint resolves
which source wins in this class of conflict: renderer and grammar decisions adopt A4
wherever A1 and A4 disagree. What remained was to record the resulting grammar as the
single binding rule — this is a Phase 0 decision, and interface.md and master-prompt.md
rows referencing titles and attribution cannot close until it is made.

The competing options were:

1. The A1 treatment of titles and attribution.
2. The A4 grammar, adopted as the single grammar for every renderer-issued surface.
3. Leave both wordings in place per surface and decide per page.

## Decision

The A4 grammar is adopted as the single grammar for all renderer-issued surfaces. **The
surface title carries module identity** — a reader looking at any surface knows from its
title which module owns it. **Developer attribution occupies the footer/subtitle
attribution slot** — credit to the module's developer is presented in the attribution
position the grammar reserves for it, never in the title. There are no per-module or
per-surface title conventions outside this grammar.

## Consequences

- **The Renderer** applies the grammar uniformly across Modes D/M/C; the static
  Components v2 frame reserves the title for module identity and the footer/subtitle slot
  for attribution, and every renderer-issued render conforms.
- **The Core Command Surface** reference set (dashboard, health, browse, queue, persona,
  permit, modules, hello-world) demonstrates the grammar; those pages are its reference
  implementations.
- **master-prompt.md** binds the personality to the grammar — voice and tone never move
  attribution into a title or displace module identity from one.
- **interface.md** documents the grammar as settled Phase 0 policy; any A1-era title
  guidance still in the corpus is superseded by this ADR.
- **Invariant:** module identity in the title, developer attribution in its slot, on every
  surface, with no exceptions — a different grammar requires a superseding ADR.
