# UR-20261026-102 — Settle the Records Into the Corpus, Then Retire the Tier

**Identity:** UR-20261026-102 · **Version:** 1.0 · **Date:** 26/10/2026 · **Requester:** operator-directed

**Freeze status:** **Open — raised 2026-10-26.** The last record in this series. It edits no frozen
record, and its own completion retires the tier it belongs to.

**Basis.** The corpus now carries four parallel record tiers beside its normative documents: 108 UR
records, 16 ADRs, a 233 KB change-log, and a 72 KB migration ledger, plus the protocol documents that
drive them. Their content overlaps the normative corpus, contradicts it in places, and is cited by it
only rarely. This record settles the durable content into the normative corpus and retires the
parallel tiers, so that one statement of a behaviour has one home.

## 1. Objective and rationale

Collapse three sources of truth — the shipped code, the normative corpus, and the record tiers — into
one declared authority order, and give every durable claim that today lives only in a record a
normative home. Then retire the record tier, keeping the audit trail in the archive rather than in
the live corpus.

The rationale is not tidiness. A reader who must consult a request record to learn how a mechanism
behaves will eventually read a record that has been overtaken by the code, and will have no way to
know. That is the failure this record exists to remove.

## 3. Implementation approach

Staged, each stage gated and separately revertable.

**Stage 1 — extract.** Work the disposition matrix
(`docs/status/consolidation-disposition.json`) record by record. For each, write the durable
normative statement into the target department, in that department's own voice and structure. Where
the department already states it, add nothing and record the citation. The matrix's `target` column is
a machine-derived first pass: an engineer confirms or corrects it before writing, and a corrected row
is a recorded decision, not a silent edit.

**Stage 2 — declare precedence.** One authority order, stated once, in the governance charter: the
specification governs required behaviour; the shipped code governs what is actually implemented; where
they differ the divergence is recorded as a code-gap in `status/code-reconciliation.json` and is never
smoothed over in prose. A record is evidence of a decision, never an authority over behaviour.

**Stage 3 — retire.** Move `upgradation/`, `adr/`, `migration-ledger.md`, `migration/`, `prompts/` and
`orchestration/` into archive/v5.0.0-records/, preserving every file. Replace them in the live corpus
with one generated index that lists each retired record, its disposition, and the document that now
carries its content. Nothing is deleted: the audit trail survives, it simply stops competing with the
corpus.

**Stage 4 — rewire the gates.** Take the 27 affected gates one at a time. A gate whose subject no
longer exists is retired with a note naming what replaced it. A gate whose subject moved is repointed.
`check-ur` becomes `check-records-index` over the generated index; `check-chain` and
`check-chain-states` retire with the chain they police; `check-supersession` retires with the ADR
series, and its supersession facts move into the departments that inherited the decisions.

**Stage 5 — reconcile the status tier.** Update `manifest.json`, `status/counts.json`,
`status/coverage-ledger.json`, `status/entities.json`, `status/section-order.json`,
`status/audit-age.json`, `status/reconciliation.json` and `_generated/` in the same change, so no
ledger names a path that no longer exists.

## 4. Acceptance criteria and definition of done

- **ACC-UR-20261026-102-1** — every one of the 124 records carries a disposition row naming its
  target document and its fate (absorbed, cited, or retired-with-pointer).
- **ACC-UR-20261026-102-2** — no durable normative claim exists only in a record: a sample audit of at
  least 25 records finds its statement present in the named target document.
- **ACC-UR-20261026-102-3** — the authority order is stated once in the governance charter, and the
  charter is the only place that states it.
- **ACC-UR-20261026-102-4** — the live corpus contains no file under `upgradation/`, `adr/`,
  `migration/`, `prompts/` or `orchestration/`, and no `migration-ledger.md` at the repository root.
- **ACC-UR-20261026-102-5** — every retired file exists under archive/v5.0.0-records/ and is listed
  in the generated index.
- **ACC-UR-20261026-102-6** — the gate suite is green at its post-retirement count, with the count and
  the retired gates named in the change-log entry.
- **ACC-UR-20261026-102-7** — `git branch --show-current` prints `feature/dev` before every commit.

## 5. Dependencies and risks

- **Risk — extraction becomes paraphrase, and paraphrase loses the decision.** Each statement is
  written to stand alone in its department, and a sample is audited against its source record
  (ACC-2). A statement that cannot be checked against its record is not landed.
- **Risk — retiring 27 gate references at once hides a real regression.** Gates are repointed in the
  same commit as the file they police, and the suite is run before and after each stage, with both
  results recorded.
- **Risk — the machine classification is wrong for some records.** The `target` column is a first
  pass; an engineer confirms every row, and a correction is recorded rather than applied silently.
- **Risk — the archive becomes a second live tier by another name.** The archive is read-only, is
  excluded from the gates that police the live corpus, and is indexed once.
- **Risk — CI cannot confirm any of this.** `OI-8`: no Actions job has started since 2026-10-23, so
  every gate result in this change is a local result and must be reported as such.

## 6. Rollback and mitigation

- One atomic commit per stage, and one per gate group inside Stage 4. `git revert` on a stage restores
  the previous structure; no stage depends on a later one having landed.
- The retirement is a move, not a delete, so restoring a tier is a move back.
- No force-push, no history rewrite, and no protected-branch change is involved.

## 7. Out of scope

- **Rewriting the corpus's normative content beyond what extraction requires.** A department is edited
  only where a record's durable claim has no home there.
- **Closing `OI-8`, the CI billing block.** Unchanged and owned by the operator.
- **Changing the shipped code.** Where extraction finds the code and the specification disagree, the
  divergence is recorded as a code-gap; fixing the code is a separate code track.
- **Retiring `archive/v4.5.1/`.** It is already quarantined and outside the gates.
